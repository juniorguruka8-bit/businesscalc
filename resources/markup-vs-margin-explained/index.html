Markup vs Margin: What Is the Difference?

Markup and margin are two ways of describing the same profit, and mixing them up is one of the most expensive vocabulary mistakes in small business. A seller who thinks they are pricing at a 30 percent margin but actually applies a 30 percent markup gives away profit on every single sale.

The Two Formulas

Markup % = (Selling Price - Cost) / Cost x 100. Markup measures profit against what the product cost you.

Margin % = (Selling Price - Cost) / Selling Price x 100. Margin measures profit against what the customer paid.

The numerator is identical. Only the denominator changes, and because the selling price is always bigger than the cost, margin is always the smaller number for the same transaction.

One Product, Two Answers

A product costs $80 and sells for $100. Profit is $20 either way.

Markup = $20 / $80 = 25 percent. Margin = $20 / $100 = 20 percent.

Now suppose you want a true 30 percent margin on an $80 cost. The price is not $80 plus 30 percent of $80 ($104). Margin pricing divides: $80 / (1 - 0.30) = $80 / 0.70 = $114.29. At $104 your margin is only about 23 percent. The Markup Calculator converts both directions so you never have to guess.

Quick Conversions Worth Knowing

A 25 percent markup equals a 20 percent margin. A 50 percent markup equals a 33.3 percent margin. A 100 percent markup, doubling your cost, equals a 50 percent margin. Notice that no markup, however large, produces a 100 percent margin: margin approaches 100 percent only as cost approaches zero.

When to Use Each

Use markup when you are building a price from a cost, because it is simple arithmetic your suppliers and staff can follow. Use margin when you are judging results, comparing products, or setting targets, because margin connects directly to your financial statements and to the share of each sales dollar you keep. Many businesses quote prices with markup and manage the business with margin; the trouble starts only when the two are treated as the same number.

The Discount Trap

The difference also matters in sales promotions. A 20 percent discount on a product priced with a 25 percent markup (20 percent margin) does not reduce your profit by a fifth; it eliminates it entirely, because the discount comes off the price while your cost stays fixed. Before any promotion, run the discounted price through the Pricing Calculator and look at the margin that remains.

A Shortcut for Converting Between Them

If you know the markup and need the margin, divide the markup by one plus the markup: a 50 percent markup becomes 0.50 / 1.50 = 33.3 percent margin. Going the other way, divide the margin by one minus the margin: a 40 percent margin needs 0.40 / 0.60 = 66.7 percent markup. Keep these two conversions near your pricing work and the confusion disappears.

One more practical tip: agree on one term inside your business. If your price lists say "margin" but your staff calculate markup, every quote carries a silent error. Write the formula you use on the price list itself, and run spot checks with the Markup Calculator whenever a new product is priced. The words are small; the money is not.
