How to Calculate Etsy Profit After Fees

Etsy fees arrive in small pieces across a monthly statement, which makes it easy to sell steadily without knowing whether a listing earns anything. The fix is to reconstruct one order completely.

The Fees in One Etsy Order

Three platform charges apply to most orders. The listing fee (widely published as USD 0.20 per listing, shown here as $0.27) is charged when an item lists and renews. The transaction fee, currently 6.5 percent, applies to the total the buyer pays, including the shipping you charge. Payment processing, commonly 3 percent plus $0.30, also applies to that same total. On top of platform fees sit your own costs: the product and the actual shipping, which is rarely the same as the shipping charged.

A Worked Example

Price $40, shipping charged $5, product cost $12, actual shipping $6, default fees.

Revenue = $40 + $5 = $45. Transaction fee = $45 x 6.5% = $2.93. Processing = $45 x 3% + $0.30 = $1.65. Listing fee = $0.27. Total fees = about $4.85.

Profit = $45 - $12 - $6 - $4.85 = about $22.16 per order, a margin near 49.2 percent. At 100 orders, that listing earns roughly $2,216. The Etsy Profit Calculator reproduces this with editable fee fields, because Etsy changes its rates from time to time and your shop's numbers should rule.

The Shipping Fee Surprise

Because the percentage fees apply to price plus shipping charged, raising your shipping charge raises your fees too, and offering "free" shipping by folding the cost into the price leaves the percentage fees roughly the same. Neither structure is automatically better; what matters is comparing the final profit per order under each, which the calculator makes a thirty-second job.

Etsy Ads Change the Answer

If you run Etsy Ads or are enrolled in offsite advertising, add the advertising cost per order. Some listings that look profitable are break-even once ads are charged to them. Others are genuinely strong and deserve the promotion. You cannot tell which is which from the sales count.

Keeping Fees Current

Platform fees change periodically, which is why the calculator never hard-codes them invisibly: every fee is an editable field prefilled with widely published defaults. Verify current fees with Etsy before making financial decisions, update the fields, and your profit figures stay honest. Then use the Profit Calculator to compare a listing against your other channels on the same basis.

Listing Fees and Renewals

The listing fee is small but easy to misjudge across a whole shop, because it is charged per listing and charged again when a listing renews, including after each sale of a multi-quantity listing and when a listing expires. A shop with many slow listings pays a steady trickle of renewal fees that never appears in any single order's profit. When you review a listing's performance, count how many listing fees it has consumed in the period, not just the one attached to its last sale, and fold that into your judgment about whether the listing earns its place.

This is also why pruning matters. Listings that sell rarely and renew often can cost more in fees than they return in profit. The per-order calculator tells you if a sale is profitable; a quick quarterly review of fees per listing tells you if the listing itself is.
