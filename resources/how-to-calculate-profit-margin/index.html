How to Calculate Profit Margin

Your profit margin answers a simple question: out of every dollar of sales, how much does your business actually keep? It is the single most useful health check for a product or a whole business, and it takes one division to calculate.

The Profit Margin Formula

Profit margin is profit divided by revenue, expressed as a percentage:

Profit Margin = (Revenue - Costs) / Revenue x 100

The profit in this formula is revenue minus the costs you choose to include. Gross margin uses only the direct costs of what you sold (the product and its delivery, for example). Net margin uses every cost of running the business. When someone quotes a margin, it is worth asking which one they mean, because the two can be far apart.

A Worked Example

Say your store takes in $1,000 of revenue in a week, and the products and their direct costs come to $600. Profit = $1,000 - $600 = $400. Margin = $400 / $1,000 x 100 = 40 percent.

Read that as: for every $100 in sales, approximately $40 remains as gross profit, before rent, wages, software and other overheads take their share. The Profit Margin Calculator does this instantly and updates that sentence with your own numbers.

Margin Is Not Markup

The most common margin mistake is confusing it with markup. Markup is profit as a percentage of cost; margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. A product that costs $80 and sells for $100 has a $20 profit: a 25 percent markup but only a 20 percent margin. Price with the wrong one and you systematically earn less than you planned. Our Markup vs Margin guide covers the difference in full.

Common Margin Mistakes

Leaving costs out. A margin calculated without shipping, fees or advertising is a fantasy number. Decide which costs belong in your margin and include them every time, the same way.

Mixing periods. Revenue from this month against costs from last month produces nonsense. Match the revenue and the costs to the same sales.

Judging one product by the store's margin. A healthy average can hide individual products that lose money. Check important products individually with the Profit Calculator.

How to Improve Your Margin

There are only three levers: raise prices, lower the costs inside the margin, or sell a richer mix of products. Small moves compound: a two point margin gain on steady sales is often worth more than a large, expensive push for more traffic. Recheck your margin whenever a supplier, platform or shipping cost changes, because margins erode silently.

Track Margin Like a Habit, Not an Event

One margin calculation tells you where you stood. A series of them tells you where you are going. Recalculate your margin monthly using the same cost definition every time, and investigate any month that moves by more than a point or two. Supplier price rises, shipping changes and a slow drift toward discounting all show up in the margin line before they show up anywhere else. Owners who watch this number catch problems while they are still small enough to fix with a price adjustment rather than a rescue plan.

It also pays to calculate margin at two levels: the whole business, for overall health, and your top products individually, because averages hide losers. A store can post a respectable overall margin while one popular product quietly loses money on every order. Finding that product is worth more than a month of marketing.
